REFLECTION

The most challenging and meaningful part of this project was building the underlying structure of the kaleidoscope. Getting the single triangular slice to behave correctly—so it could be clipped, mirrored, and rotated into a seamless hexagonal tiling—required a deeper understanding of geometry, clipping masks, and transformation logic than I initially expected. I relied heavily on studying kaleidoscope by joshlory (https://editor.p5js.org/joshlory/sketches/rkNOw8y1E), which helped me understand how to use the drawTriangle and drawHex functions together to form the repeating pattern. Reconstructing this foundation from scratch taught me how sensitive generative visuals are to structure; a single misalignment or missing push/pop can distort the entire pattern.

Once the triangular grid was stable, I explored ways to give the kaleidoscope a more “trippy,” hypnotic aesthetic. Integrating orbiting planets—based on the solar system sketch by eevirutanen (https://editor.p5js.org/eevirutanen/sketches/lU4z6KeKo)—added rhythmic motion that multiplied beautifully through the tessellated grid. I also incorporated a procedural snowflake inspired by Computational Mama’s tutorial (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2gaYq1-XZPE), which introduced an organic, fractal layer that contrasted the geometric orbits.

During the final critique, I learned how important user experience is when designing interactive systems. My classmates’ feedback highlighted the need for both curation and customization. Users appreciated the ability to personalize colors, but also benefited from curated, cohesive color themes that ensured the kaleidoscope always remained visually harmonious. This led me to develop a theme system with coordinated palettes, while also allowing adjustments like background color changes for greater flexibility. Balancing aesthetic control with user freedom gave me insight into designing tools that are structured enough to produce reliable outcomes, yet open enough to invite play and exploration.

Overall, this project strengthened my understanding of computational geometry, interactivity, and the user experience considerations required for creative coding tools.
